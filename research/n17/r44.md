# Uniqueness of the 17-vertex (4,4)-Ramsey graph

Date: 2026-10-21. Author: `UniquenessProof`.

This note gives an elementary uniqueness proof, including complete coverage of
all local signatures. Its claims are **ready for independent review**, not
accepted. Search use is **none pending review**. There is no novelty claim.
No graph catalogue, solver output, or classification theorem is a premise.
No Lean file is edited by this note's author.

The only smaller Ramsey bound used is R(3,4) <= 9. The existing
[census-free parameter proof](../n43/d18/large_row_extensions.md#claim-seventeen-codegree-moments)
is also a source for Section 1. The argument below restates the needed proof so
that the uniqueness argument can be reviewed independently of its branch use.

Throughout, graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. A (4,4)-Ramsey graph has
neither a 4-clique nor an independent 4-set. Indices are in Z/8Z unless a
different range is specified. Write W for the Wagner graph on these indices,
with adjacency differences 1, 4, and 7. An antipodal pair is {i,i+4}. A
rim edge is {i,i+1}. The eight distance-3 pairs are {i,i+3}; these are distinct
as unordered pairs.

## 1. The parameters and the local Wagner graph

Claim ID: `research/n17/r44.md#claim-r44-parameters`
Kind: local-deduction
Review state: ready
Scope: every (4,4)-Ramsey graph G of order 17
Dependencies: R(3,4) <= 9
Conclusion: G is SRG(17,8,3,4), and every open neighborhood in G or its complement is W
Search use: none pending review
Reviews: none

For every vertex x, its neighborhood is triangle-free and independent-4-free,
so has order at most 8. Its nonneighborhood is clique-4-free and
independent-3-free, so also has order at most 8. The two orders sum to 16.
Consequently G is 8-regular, and so is its complement.

An edge's common neighborhood is independent, hence has order at most 3.
For a nonedge xy with c common neighbors, the common nonneighborhood has order
15-(16-c)=c-1. It is a clique, so c <= 4. There are 68 edges and 68 nonedges,
and the sum of all unordered-pair codegrees is

```text
17 * binom(8,2) = 476 = 68*3 + 68*4.
```

Every upper bound must therefore be an equality. This proves the stated SRG
parameters. In particular, every local graph G[N(x)] is cubic, triangle-free,
and independent-4-free, on 8 vertices. The same holds in the complement.

Here is a complete elementary classification of just this cubic local class.
Let H be a cubic, triangle-free, independent-4-free graph on 8 vertices.
Choose x. Its 3 neighbors p,q,r are independent. Let U be the other 4 vertices.
The graph H[U] has no independent 3-set, because x is anticomplete to U.
It is triangle-free and has maximum degree at most 2: three neighbors of a
vertex in U would be independent. The 3 vertices p,q,r each have 2 neighbors
in U, and the degree sum in U is 12, so H[U] has (12-6)/2=3 edges.
A triangle-free graph on 4 vertices with 3 edges and maximum degree at most 2
is a path. Write its vertices in order as u0,u1,u2,u3.

Each of p,q,r has 2 nonadjacent neighbors on this path. The only possible
pairs are {u0,u2}, {u0,u3}, {u1,u3}. Vertex u1 has exactly 1 neighbor among
p,q,r, as does u2. Therefore the first and third pairs occur once each, and
the second pair also occurs once. This determines H. To identify it explicitly
with W, use the labels

```text
x=0;  u0=3, u1=2, u2=6, u3=5;
{u0,u2}-vertex=7; {u0,u3}-vertex=4; {u1,u3}-vertex=1.
```

These are exactly the edges of W.

**Important restriction.** This does not say that all (3,4;8) graphs are
cubic or isomorphic. For example C8 together with just the antipodal edges
{0,4} and {1,5} is triangle-free and independent-4-free but not cubic. The
only independent 4-sets of C8 are its two alternating classes, and the two
added edges destroy them. Cubicity here is supplied by the 17-vertex SRG
parameters, not by the (3,4;8) condition alone.

## 2. An explicit 38-signature domain with complete coverage

Claim ID: `research/n17/r44.md#claim-r44-signature-domain`
Kind: local-deduction
Review state: ready
Scope: 4-subsets S of V(W) whose complements contain no independent 3-set
Dependencies: the explicit adjacency relation of W
Conclusion: exactly the 38 sets in the five families below satisfy the condition
Search use: none pending review
Reviews: none

The families are

```text
C_i = {i,i+1,i+2,i+3}                   (8 sets)
L_i = {i-1,i,i+1,i+3}                   (8 sets)
R_i = {i,i+2,i+3,i+4}                   (8 sets)
S_i = {i-1,i,i+1,i+4}                   (8 sets, a closed star)
Q_j = {j,j+1,j+4,j+5}, 0 <= j < 4       (4 sets, a 4-cycle)
E   = {0,2,4,6}, O = {1,3,5,7}         (2 sets, each 2 disjoint edges).
```

The mixed family M means the 16 sets L_i,R_i. The closed-star family is denoted
S only in this section's family notation, not as a generic signature.
The 38 sets are pairwise distinct. The following tables give a complete
70-case coverage proof, without relying on an unspecified case split.
Encode a subset by the integer sum of 2^i over its vertices.

### No antipodal pair: 16 cases

Such a set chooses exactly 1 vertex from each of the 4 antipodal pairs.
Exactly the following 8 are legal:

```text
15, 30, 60, 120, 135, 195, 225, 240.
```

These are C_0 through C_7. Their complements are also 4-vertex paths, so have
no independent 3-set. The other 8 cases are excluded by these explicit
independent triples in their complements:

| Mask | Independent triple in the complement |
| ---: | --- |
| 45 | {1,4,6} |
| 75 | {2,4,7} |
| 90 | {0,2,5} |
| 105 | {1,4,7} |
| 150 | {0,3,5} |
| 165 | {1,3,6} |
| 180 | {0,3,6} |
| 210 | {0,2,5} |

### Exactly 1 antipodal pair: 48 cases

Rotate the unique antipodal pair to {0,4}. The other 2 vertices form one of
the following 12 pairs. Rotating this table back covers 4*12=48 different
sets, because their unique antipodal pair determines the applicable table.

| Other pair | Outcome or independent triple in the complement |
| --- | --- |
| {1,2} | legal, L_1 |
| {1,3} | excluded, {2,5,7} |
| {1,6} | excluded, {2,5,7} |
| {1,7} | legal, S_0 |
| {2,3} | legal, R_0 |
| {2,5} | excluded, {1,3,6} |
| {2,7} | excluded, {1,3,6} |
| {3,5} | legal, S_4 |
| {3,6} | excluded, {2,5,7} |
| {5,6} | legal, L_5 |
| {5,7} | excluded, {1,3,6} |
| {6,7} | legal, R_4 |

The complements of the 6 legal sets are 4-vertex paths. This can be checked
directly from the listed adjacency differences. Thus the table proves both
directions, not just exclusion of unwanted sets. There are 16 mixed sets
and 8 closed stars.

### Exactly 2 antipodal pairs: 6 cases

Choose 2 of the 4 antipodal pairs. Their indices in {0,1,2,3} give

| Pair indices | Mask | Family |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| {0,1} | 51 | Q_0 |
| {0,2} | 85 | E |
| {0,3} | 153 | Q_3 |
| {1,2} | 102 | Q_1 |
| {1,3} | 170 | O |
| {2,3} | 204 | Q_2 |

Every complement is either a 4-cycle or 2 disjoint edges, so all 6 are legal.
There are no other cases: the antipodal-pair counts 0,1,2 partition all
4-subsets, with 16+48+6=70 cases.

## 3. The pair-incidence equations force exactly L or exactly R

Claim ID: `research/n17/r44.md#claim-r44-incidence-rigidity`
Kind: local-deduction
Review state: ready
Scope: any multiset of 8 legal signatures from Section 2
Dependencies: the complete signature domain and the incidence equations below
Conclusion: the multiset consists of all 8 L_i once each, or all 8 R_i once each
Search use: none pending review
Reviews: none

Assume every unordered pair of indices occurs in exactly 2 signatures,
except each distance-3 pair {i,i+3}, which occurs in exactly 1. No
distinctness assumption on the multiset is needed.

The following table records the number of pairs of each cyclic distance
inside 1 signature. It also supplies every aggregate identity used below.

| Family | Distance 1 | Distance 2 | Distance 3 | Distance 4 | W-edges |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| C | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 3 |
| M | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 3 |
| S | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 3 |
| Q | 2 | 0 | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| E or O | 0 | 4 | 0 | 2 | 2 |
| Required total | 16 | 16 | 8 | 8 | 24 |

Let c,m,s,q,r be the multiplicity totals of C,M,S,Q,{E,O}, respectively.
All are nonnegative integers and c+m+s+q+r=8. The W-edge column gives q=r.
The distance-1 column, after subtracting twice the total-signature equation,
gives c=2r. The distance-3 column gives s+q=r, hence s=0. Consequently

```text
q=r, c=2r, s=0, m=8-4r, and r is 0, 1, or 2.
```

Every C,M,Q signature has a distance-3 pair. Since such a pair must occur
exactly once, each such individual signature occurs at most once.

For use in eliminating r>0, take the alternating sum of the 8 rim-edge
incidence equations. A C_i contributes +1 for even i and -1 for odd i.
Every mixed signature contributes 0, because its 2 rim edges have opposite
parities. A Q_j contributes +2 for even j and -2 for odd j. E and O contribute
0. Therefore

```text
(number of even C) - (number of odd C)
  + 2*((number of even Q) - (number of odd Q)) = 0.             (A)
```

Each C_i contains the unique distance-3 pair with index i. Each L_i or R_i
contains the unique distance-3 pair with index i. Q_j contains exactly the
2 distance-3 pairs with indices j+1 and j+5.

### Case r=1 is impossible

Here q=1, c=2, m=4. Rotate so that the unique Q is Q_0. Equation (A) forces
both C signatures to have odd indices. Q_0 already occupies distance-3
indices 1 and 5, so those C signatures must be C_3 and C_7. The 4 mixed
signatures must have the remaining indices 0,2,4,6, with exactly 1 of L_i,R_i
for each of these indices.

Rim edge {0,1} already occurs in Q_0 and C_7. Thus it cannot occur in a mixed
signature. Among the available mixed signatures it occurs in L_0 and R_6,
so those are excluded. Similarly rim edge {4,5} is already in Q_0 and C_3,
excluding L_4 and R_2. The 4 mixed signatures are therefore forced to be

```text
R_0, L_2, R_4, L_6.
```

But Q_0, R_0, and R_4 all contain the antipodal pair {0,4}. Its required
incidence is 2, not at least 3. This is a contradiction.

### Case r=2 is impossible

Here q=2, c=4, m=0. Each Q contains an antipodal pair of even base index and
an antipodal pair of odd base index. If E occurred twice, both even-base
antipodal pairs would already have incidence 2, leaving no room for any Q.
Likewise O cannot occur twice. Thus E and O each occur once.

Every antipodal pair now has only 1 incidence remaining for the 2 Q
signatures. Regard Q_j as the edge {j,j+1} on the 4 antipodal-pair indices.
The 2 Q signatures must be disjoint edges of this 4-cycle. Hence they are
Q_0,Q_2 or Q_1,Q_3. In particular they have the same parity.

Equation (A) forces all 4 C signatures to have the opposite parity. But the
2 Q signatures already occupy all 4 distance-3 pairs of precisely that
opposite parity. No such C signature can be present. This is a contradiction.

### Case r=0 gives exactly the 2 stated solutions

Only mixed signatures remain. Each of the 8 distance-3 pairs occurs once,
so for each i exactly 1 of L_i and R_i is selected. Put b_i=1 if L_i is
selected and b_i=0 if R_i is selected.

Rim edge {j,j+1} occurs among mixed signatures precisely in
L_j,L_(j+1),R_(j-2),R_(j-3). Its required incidence 2 gives

```text
b_j + b_(j+1) = b_(j-2) + b_(j-3).
```

Set d_j=b_j+b_(j+1). This says d_j=d_(j-3). Addition of 3 cycles through all
8 indices, in the explicit order 0,3,6,1,4,7,2,5. Thus all d_j are equal.
Subtracting consecutive equalities gives b_j=b_(j+2).

The antipodal pair {j,j+4} occurs precisely in
L_(j+1),L_(j+5),R_j,R_(j+4). Its incidence 2 gives

```text
b_(j+1) + b_(j+5) = b_j + b_(j+4).
```

The established period 2 reduces this to 2*b_(j+1)=2*b_j. Therefore all b_i
are equal. All are 0 or all are 1, proving the claim, including multiplicities.

## 4. Applying incidence rigidity and reconstructing every edge

Claim ID: `research/n17/r44.md#claim-r44-paley-uniqueness`
Kind: local-deduction
Review state: ready
Scope: every (4,4)-Ramsey graph G on 17 vertices
Dependencies: Sections 1-3 and the explicit mod-17 adjacency table below
Conclusion: G is isomorphic to the Paley graph on Z/17Z
Search use: none pending review
Reviews: none

Choose v. Label its 8 neighbors a_i so that their graph is W. Let B be its
8 nonneighbors. For b in B define its signature

```text
T_b = {i : a_i is adjacent to b}.
```

The SRG nonedge codegree of v,b is 4, so |T_b|=4. Its complement contains no
independent 3-set in W, since those 3 vertices together with b would form an
independent 4-set in G. Therefore T_b lies in the explicit 38-set domain.

For distinct a_i,a_j, their common neighbors include v, their common
neighbors inside W, and the B vertices whose signatures contain {i,j}.
For W, the common-neighbor count is 0 at distances 1 and 4, 1 at distance 2,
and 2 at distance 3. Subtract these and v from the SRG codegrees:

| Cyclic distance | G adjacency | G codegree | Common in W | Incidence in B |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | edge | 3 | 0 | 2 |
| 2 | nonedge | 4 | 1 | 2 |
| 3 | nonedge | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| 4 | edge | 3 | 0 | 2 |

This is exactly Section 3's hypothesis. Thus the signatures are all L_i or
all R_i, with each occurring exactly once. Label the unique B vertex of
signature L_i or R_i as b_i.

Reflection i -> -i on the A labels takes R_i to L_(-i-3). Relabel the B
vertices accordingly. Hence it suffices to treat the all-L case. We now have

```text
v-a_i: always an edge;       v-b_i: always a nonedge;
a_i-a_j: differences 1,4,7;
a_j-b_i: j-i in {7,0,1,3}.
```

It remains to determine the B graph, not merely the signatures. Each b_i has
4 A neighbors and total degree 8, so it has exactly 4 B neighbors. For i=0,

```text
L_0 intersect L_1 = {0,1};
L_0 intersect L_4 = {3,7};
L_0 intersect L_7 = {7,0}.
```

Each displayed pair is an edge of W. Thus b_0 cannot be adjacent to b_1,b_4,
or b_7, because that edge and the displayed A edge would form K4. Rotating
the argument, b_i cannot meet b_(i+1),b_(i+4),b_(i+7). Only 4 possible B
neighbors remain, and its B degree is 4. Therefore

```text
b_i-b_j is an edge exactly at differences 2,3,5,6.
```

This reconstructs every edge of G.

### Explicit Paley labeling

Let Q={1,2,4,8,9,13,15,16} be the nonzero squares modulo 17. Define

```text
v   -> 0;
a_i -> 2^i;
b_i -> 10*2^i,                  all arithmetic modulo 17.
```

The powers and the B labels in index order are

```text
2^i:     1, 2, 4, 8,16,15,13, 9;
10*2^i: 10, 3, 6,12, 7,14,11, 5.
```

The first list is exactly Q. The second is exactly its 8 nonzero nonsquares.
Together with 0, this is a bijection to Z/17Z. The following complete
difference table checks adjacency, with a dash for a zero difference.

| d | 2^d | 2^d-1 | Square? | 10-2^d | Square? |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| 0 | 1 | 0 | no, diagonal | 9 | yes |
| 1 | 2 | 1 | yes | 8 | yes |
| 2 | 4 | 3 | no | 6 | no |
| 3 | 8 | 7 | no | 2 | yes |
| 4 | 16 | 15 | yes | 11 | no |
| 5 | 15 | 14 | no | 12 | no |
| 6 | 13 | 12 | no | 14 | no |
| 7 | 9 | 8 | yes | 1 | yes |

Multiplying a difference by 2^i preserves square status. Multiplying a
nonzero difference by 10 reverses square status. Thus the table gives
A adjacency at differences 1,4,7, B adjacency at differences 2,3,5,6, and
cross adjacency at offsets 7,0,1,3, exactly as reconstructed. Edges from 0
are exactly to the A labels. The displayed bijection is therefore the desired
isomorphism. This proves the uniqueness statement without an external census.

## 5. Small kernel-checkable predicates and the actual computation scope

Claim ID: `research/n17/r44.md#claim-r44-finite-predicates`
Kind: design
Review state: ready
Scope: implementation of the explicit finite facts in Sections 2-4
Dependencies: the preceding human proof
Conclusion: the finite obligations can be expressed without enumerating 17-vertex graphs
Search use: none pending review
Reviews: none

Section 2 may be checked with a Boolean predicate on n in {0,...,255}:

```text
Allowed(n) := popcount(n)=4 and
  for every 0<=i<j<k<8 outside n,
  W(i,j) or W(i,k) or W(j,k).

Masks := {
  15,23,29,30,39,46,51,57,58,60,71,78,85,92,102,113,114,116,120,
  135,139,142,147,153,156,163,170,184,195,197,201,204,209,225,
  226,228,232,240
}.

For every n<256, Allowed(n) iff n is in Masks.
```

The signature-incidence lemma can be stated with 38 natural variables x_n,
not Boolean variables. Its hypotheses are sum x_n=8 and, for every i<j,

```text
sum_{n in Masks, i and j in n} x_n =
  if j-i is 3 or 5 then 1 else 2.
```

Its conclusion is that the nonzero variables, each equal to 1, have masks

```text
L: {23,46,92,113,139,184,197,226}, or
R: {29,58,71,116,142,163,209,232}.
```

Section 3 is a complete elementary proof of this 38-variable statement.
A kernel-checked linear integer arithmetic proof is another implementation;
no trusted evaluation or solver axiom is needed. In particular, duplicate
signature exclusion need not be a separate graph-theoretic lemma.

For independent reproducibility of the finite arithmetic exploration only:
a plain exact-rational row reduction of the total, vertex, and pair equations
has rank 28 on the 38 columns. Aggregate identities first eliminate all 8
star columns. The remaining 30-column system has rank 20. With columns sorted
by increasing mask its 10 free-column masks are

```text
116,120,170,184,204,209,225,226,232,240.
```

The nonmatching free variables are bounded by 1 by their distance-3 pairs.
Matching 170 is bounded by 2 by an antipodal pair. Checking the resulting
3*2^9=1536 exact assignments gave precisely the two stated solutions. This
was a small arithmetic exploration, not an independent review or formal
certificate, and is not a premise of the human proof. No graph catalogue,
solver, build, test suite, download, or installation was run. The only local
computations were enumerating the 70 four-subsets, tabulating their explicit
witnesses, and reducing this tiny rational incidence system.
