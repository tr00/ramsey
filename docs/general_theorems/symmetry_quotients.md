# Exact residual symmetry and arbitrary-domain descent

Date: 2026-10-15. Task: symmetry-extensions-20261015. Author: Codex.

These mathematical deductions extend the accepted
[column-multiset symmetry note](../../n43/d18/symmetry_reduction.md).
All claims are **ready for independent review**, with an author's self-check
only. They are not accepted pruning rules, implemented algorithms or
certificates. No graph search, enumeration, solver, canonicalizer, package,
download or literature review was used. No novelty claim is made.

## Setting and conventions

Fix a finite labeled graph A and a multiset M of subsets C of V(A).
Give the occurrences of each C ranks j=1,...,m_C, so the B occurrence
set is {(C,j):m_C>0, 1<=j<=m_C}. The order of all occurrences and the
order e_1,...,e_L of all unordered B pairs are fixed once and for all.
An edge vector z has bit z_e on pair e, with 0<1. A permutation tau
acts by (tau.z)_e=z_(tau^-1(e)). All extra assigned data must transform
with their indices. Use

```text
H={g in Aut(A):m_(gC)=m_C for every C},
K=product_C S_(m_C),
Gamma={(g,tau):tau(C,j) is an occurrence of gC for every (C,j)}.
```

A feasible full-vector family F must be invariant under the group used.
This includes full Ramsey completions of fixed A,M when every stored
condition transforms consistently. None of the group arguments depends
on a Ramsey edge census, the pivot degree or the forbidden-set sizes.

## 1. The residual extension splits, and quotient minimization composes

Claim ID: `docs/general_theorems/symmetry_quotients.md#claim-symmetry-quotients-01`

Kind: local-deduction

Review state: ready

Scope: the complete residual action in the setting; arbitrary multiplicities.

Dependencies: [d18 symmetry Claim 01](../../n43/d18/symmetry_reduction.md#claim-d18-symmetry-01)
for the existing exact sequence; the proof below also derives the splitting directly.

Conclusion: the exact sequence splits after occurrence ranks are chosen:

```text
Gamma is isomorphic to K semidirect H,
ell(g)(C,j)=(gC,j).
```

Here H permutes the factors S_(m_C) by C->gC. Define
Can_K(z)=min_lex{k.z:k in K}. Then

```text
Can_Gamma(z)=min_(g in H) Can_K(ell(g).z).
```

This gives an exact second-stage quotient, including row automorphisms,
from an exact kernel minimizer. It does not replace minimization over H
by comparisons only against generators of H.

Proof: Equality of multiplicities makes ell(g) well-defined. Ranks are
unchanged, so ell(g)ell(h)=ell(gh), and the pair (g,ell(g)) is in Gamma.
The projection to H composed with this lift is the identity. Any
(g,tau) in Gamma factors uniquely as (1,k)(g,ell(g)), since
k=tau ell(g)^-1 preserves each signature class. Conjugating a
within-C permutation by ell(g) gives the same rank permutation within
gC. This proves the semidirect product and the factorization. Taking
the minimum over all k,g proves the displayed canonical-vector formula.
The possibly nonfaithful occurrence action of H causes no problem:
different elements can yield the same B vector while acting differently
on A. The formula is about the full group orbit, not its cardinality.

Search use: symmetry after independent review and certified implementation.

## 2. Disjoint duplicate swaps admit exact minimization by binary implications

Claim ID: `docs/general_theorems/symmetry_quotients.md#claim-symmetry-quotients-02`

Kind: local-deduction

Review state: ready

Scope: arbitrary full edge vectors on b vertices, with p specified disjoint
vertex pairs and all other vertices fixed; no Ramsey assumptions.

Dependencies: finite lexicographic order; the standard implication-graph
characterization of finite 2-CNF satisfiability, recalled below.

Conclusion: for K=(C2)^p generated by those pair swaps, its exact least
edge vector can be found using at most L=C(b,2) feasibility questions on
2-CNF formulas with p variables. No enumeration of all 2^p swaps is
required. This minimizes over **every combination** of swaps and gives
one edge vector per K orbit. Different minimizing permutations may remain.

Proof: Let s_j be the indicator of swapping pair j and let tau_s be
the product of the chosen swaps. For each coordinate e_i, define

```text
f_i(s)=(tau_s.z)_(e_i).
```

If both endpoints are fixed, or they are the two vertices of the same
pair, f_i is constant. If just one endpoint belongs to a pair it is
unary. If the endpoints belong to different pairs j,k it depends only
on s_j,s_k. Thus f_i is a Boolean function of at most two variables.
For b_i in {0,1}, the condition f_i(s)=b_i is a 2-CNF: for each
assignment a to those at most two variables that gives the wrong value,
insert the clause excluding a. This is a disjunction of at most two
literals s_j!=a_j. A constant wrong value produces an empty clause;
a constant correct value produces no clauses. At most four clauses
are needed per coordinate condition.

Start with Psi_0=true. At coordinate i test feasibility of
Psi_(i-1) AND (f_i=0). If feasible choose b_i=0; otherwise choose
b_i=1. Set Psi_i=Psi_(i-1) AND (f_i=b_i). By induction Psi_i is
feasible: Psi_(i-1) is feasible and all its assignments give either
zero or one at i. It describes exactly the orientations giving the
chosen prefix. Each chosen bit is the smallest attainable among
orientations matching earlier choices, so (b_1,...,b_L) is the exact
orbit minimum. An assignment satisfying Psi_L supplies the explicit
product-of-swaps witness.

For completeness, a 2-CNF can be decided by the implication graph:
(a OR b) gives edges not-a->b and not-b->a; a unit clause has its
single implication; an empty clause is immediately infeasible. It is
infeasible exactly when a variable and its negation lie in the same
strongly connected component. The forward direction follows by
propagating both implications. In the reverse direction, topologically
number the strongly connected components of the implication DAG so
every intercomponent edge goes forward. Assign literal a true exactly
when the component of a comes after the component of not-a. This
assigns each complementary pair consistently. An unsatisfied clause
(a OR b) would give both comp(a)<comp(not-a) and
comp(b)<comp(not-b), while its implications give
comp(not-a)<=comp(b) and comp(not-b)<=comp(a), a contradiction.
This is a mathematical algorithm; no SAT solver is invoked here.

Rebuilding each feasibility graph has O(p+i) vertices-plus-edges at
step i, hence a straightforward bound O(L*p+L^2) time and O(p+L)
space for minimization. The bound concerns this group operation only,
not the Ramsey completion search or minimization over H.

The improvement over generator comparisons is strict even with two
pairs. On vertices 0,...,4 let the pairs be {0,1},{2,3} and take

```text
E(z)={01,03,04,12,23}.
```

In lexicographic pair order, swapping either pair alone raises bit 02
from zero to one, so both generator comparisons accept z. Swapping
both leaves 01,02,03 unchanged and lowers 04 from one to zero. Thus
z is not least in its K orbit. This example illustrates the group
claim only; it is not an order-43 construction or a feasible branch.

Search use: exact kernel symmetry after independent review and validation;
not an implemented solver or automorphism procedure.

## 3. Uniform descent prunes arbitrary assigned edge domains

Claim ID: `docs/general_theorems/symmetry_quotients.md#claim-symmetry-quotients-03`

Kind: local-deduction

Review state: ready

Scope: any invariant finite family F of full bit vectors, a fixed group
Gamma and full coordinate order, and any partial assignment P on any domain D.

Dependencies: finite orbit minima; group action in the setting.

Conclusion: P may be rejected if one can prove, for some gamma in Gamma,

```text
for every z in F extending P: gamma.z <lex z.
```

Every orbit's least full vector avoids every such rejection. Consequently
these rejections may be combined throughout a tree that branches on
arbitrary edges in an arbitrary state-dependent order, provided its
ordinary branches cover all assignments and Gamma,F and the comparison
order are the same throughout. Gamma need not preserve D. There is no
requirement that D be an initial segment.

A simple directly checkable witness consists of gamma and a coordinate
i such that:

```text
For every j<i, either gamma^-1(e_j)=e_j,
  or e_j and gamma^-1(e_j) are assigned equal bits in P.
At j=i, both are assigned and
  P_(gamma^-1(e_i))=0 < 1=P_(e_i).
```

Proof: The witness proves equality at every earlier coordinate for
every extension, followed by a strict decrease at i. A least vector
z* of any full Gamma orbit cannot extend P: invariance puts gamma.z*
in the same feasible orbit, contradicting minimality. This same z*
avoids all witnessed rejections, regardless of the assigned domains
or branching order. At every ordinary complete split its matching
child exists, so there is a surviving path to it. The more general
universally quantified condition gives the same proof.

For example let Gamma exchange coordinates 1 and 3 and fix coordinate
2. The partial assignment P_1=1,P_3=0 has the witness i=1, although
coordinate 2 is unassigned and the assigned domain is not a prefix.
Even domain preservation is unnecessary: let Gamma also exchange
coordinates 2 and 4, and let P fix 1=1,3=0,2=0 but leave 4 unassigned.
The same witness works although Gamma does not preserve D.

This condition is stronger than minimizing the locally assigned vector:
it proves descent in the **one fixed full order for every extension**.
Changing that order between nodes, restricting F by noninvariant fixed
data, or merely finding some nonminimal extension does not meet the
hypotheses. At a node, gamma may be chosen from a subgroup of the
one global Gamma; it need not preserve earlier partial tests, because
the least full Gamma vector satisfies them all. Ordinary logical
pruning must also be sound. If several
data stages are minimized, use the residual stabilizer after fixing
earlier stages, or compare one full hierarchical code as in Claim 04.

Search use: symmetry after independent review; permutation and prefix
witnesses can be checked without trusting a canonicalizer. Computational
coverage still needs a deterministic tree and checked branch/witness log.

Reviews: none.

## 4. Hierarchical minimization safely composes breakers and root selection

Claim ID: `docs/general_theorems/symmetry_quotients.md#claim-symmetry-quotients-04`

Kind: local-deduction

Review state: ready

Scope: a finite group action on a finite family of complete structured
objects, with equivariant data maps t_1,...,t_r to totally ordered sets
equipped with group actions (the orders need not be action-invariant);
optionally a nonempty isomorphism-equivariant family of admissible roots.

Dependencies: group stabilizers and finite minima; Claims 01--03 as optional
ways of computing stages, not assumptions of the abstract statement.

Conclusion: minimize t_1 over an orbit, fix its chosen value, minimize
t_2 within that fiber using the stabilizer of the fixed earlier data,
and continue. This gives exactly the least tuple (t_1,...,t_r) over
the original orbit. The tuple gives a complete orbit code if it
determines a complete labeled object. For rooted Ramsey generation,
an exact rooted code can be constructed by choosing an exact labeled A
representative, a least full M under Aut(A), and a least B vector under
Gamma_M. Minimize that rooted code over a nonempty invariant admissible
root family to obtain a complete unrooted code within the branch.

Proof: After minimizing t_1, any two orbit members with this same
literal first data value differ by an element stabilizing that value.
More generally if two members have identical first i data values,
their connecting group element fixes those values, by equivariance.
Their fiber is therefore exactly one orbit of the simultaneous
stabilizer. Minimizing the next datum within the fiber is precisely
lexicographic tuple minimization. Induction proves the statement.
When the final tuple specifies the full labeled object, equal minimum
tuples are equivalent if and only if their objects are in the same orbit.

For rooted graphs, an isomorphism fixes v and maps A=N(v) and B=M(v)
to their respective parts. After choosing the same exact A code,
the accepted two-stage quotient identifies their rooted isomorphism
class exactly. For unrooted graphs an isomorphism carries every
admissible root to an admissible root with equal rooted code. Conversely
an equality between the minimum rooted codes provides a rooted
isomorphism and hence an unrooted one. This proves both directions.

The minimum code need not choose a unique vertex: automorphic roots
can tie, and all tied concrete roots must be retained or deduplicated
with checked isomorphisms. Nonautomorphic roots cannot tie when the
rooted code is complete. A mere scalar root key leaves that distinction
unresolved. An x136 branch must minimize over degree-18 roots with
x_v=136, not over all degree-18 roots. A pair-gluing branch similarly
uses its proved nonempty admissible pair family, with endpoint ordering
and all tied pair types handled in the code.

Complements can be an earlier stage only with a coverage proof for
their selector. Independent stage minima under the original group do
not have this stabilizer/fiber proof. The existing degree-sequence
selector and its canonical minimum-degree split remain intact.

Search use: symmetry and canonical root coverage after independent review
and complete computation/certification of the codes; no such code is
implemented here.

Reviews: none.

## Author's self-check and limits

The lift was checked by explicit composition and by the unique k*ell(g)
factorization. The minimizer was checked by feasibility induction; the
two-pair counterexample was calculated directly at its first differing
coordinates. Descent coverage was rederived from a least full orbit
member, not from a minimum on changing local domains. The hierarchy
argument uses equality of literal earlier data and its stabilizer.
These are author checks, not independent acceptance.

The occurrence action of H can be nonfaithful; kernel minimization alone
does not remove H, and rooted minimization alone does not remove the
choice of root. No timing benefit, full generator coverage, proof trace,
new Ramsey bound or literature novelty follows from these statements.
